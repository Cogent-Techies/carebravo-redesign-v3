What owners ask about billing.

Why is my home care agency's AR aging climbing?

AR climbs when claim problems are found after submission instead of before it. A visit that never verified, a unit count past the authorization, a missing signature — each becomes a denial weeks later, and each denial waits for someone to have time. CareBravo checks every eligible claim against the verified visit and the authorization before it goes out, so most of those denials never exist, and reviews every visible denial that does.

How do I appeal a Medicaid claim denial?

Read the denial reason on the remittance, fix the underlying record (the visit, the units, the signature, the authorization), and resubmit or appeal inside the payer's timely-filing window. Most denials are never worked: 89% of Medicaid managed-care prior-authorization denials are never appealed (HHS OIG, 2023, cited by KFF). On CareBravo, correctable claims are corrected and resubmitted once, at no charge, and the original claim is counted once.

What exactly does the 99% guarantee cover?

On the Managed Billing plan, we guarantee that at least 99% of eligible original claims are prepared and transmitted without an error we cause. Corrections and resubmissions are included and never billed twice, and detectable problems are flagged before submission. Medicaid's decision to approve or pay a claim is expressly not guaranteed — no honest vendor can guarantee payer adjudication.

Does CareBravo bill Medicaid waivers, MCOs, and other payers?

Yes. CareBravo bills Medicaid waiver programs, Medicaid managed care organizations, Medicare-certified home health where applicable, VA contracts, long-term care insurance, and private pay from the same record, so the whole payer mix runs on one claims process.

I have eight patients and do my own billing at night. Is this for an agency my size?

Yes, and the price is simpler at your size than the plans make it look. Under about 17 active clients, CareBravo Core and CareBravo Autonomous both land on the $250 monthly floor, so the question isn’t cost — it’s whether you keep doing the billing yourself with full visibility, or let it run inside your rules while you’re on visits. Managed Billing is for when the billing is worth handing to a person: our billing specialists prepare and submit every eligible claim behind the written 99% guarantee. Move up when the work is worth handing over, not before.

I run 90 patients across three MCO contracts with an in-house biller. What does Managed Billing change?

Accountability with a number attached. At your census the work is already being done; what is usually missing is a weekly, dated view of it by payer — first-pass rate, denials with causes, and AR aging for each contract — and a written 99% guarantee on the part of the process we control. Your biller keeps the payer relationships and the judgment calls; our billing specialists carry preparation, submission, and the correction cycle, counted once per claim. The authorization-to-bill cycle becomes a number you can state, which is the first thing an MCO — or a buyer — asks for.
